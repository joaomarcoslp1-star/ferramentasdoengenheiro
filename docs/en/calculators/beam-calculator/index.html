How to solve a statically determinate beam

A simply supported beam (pin + roller) or a cantilever (one fixed end) has exactly three unknown support reactions, so the three equilibrium equations are enough to find them:

1. Replace distributed loads by their resultants. A uniform load w over a length a becomes a force w·a at the middle of that length. A triangular load becomes w·a/2 at one third of the length from the larger end.

2. Sum of moments about one support = 0. This gives the reaction at the other support directly, with no simultaneous equations.

3. Sum of vertical forces = 0. This gives the remaining reaction. For a cantilever, use ΣF = 0 for the vertical reaction and ΣM = 0 at the wall for the fixed-end moment.

4. Walk along the beam. Shear V(x) is the sum of forces to the left of the section; bending moment M(x) is the sum of their moments about the section. The moment peaks where the shear crosses zero.

Worked example

The default example: a 20 ft simply supported beam with a 5 kip point load at 6 ft and a uniform load of 0.4 kip/ft over the full span.

Resultant of the distributed load: 0.4 × 20 = 8 kip at x = 10 ft.
Moments about A: RB × 20 = 5 × 6 + 8 × 10 = 110 → RB = 5.5 kip.
Vertical forces: RA = 5 + 8 − 5.5 → RA = 7.5 kip.
Maximum moment: the shear drops from 7.5 kip to 7.5 − 0.4 × 6 = 5.1 kip just left of the point load and to 0.1 kip just right of it, then reaches zero 0.25 ft further, at x = 6.25 ft. Mmax = 7.5 × 6.25 − 0.4 × 6.25²/2 − 5 × 0.25 ≈ 37.8 kip·ft.

From bending moment to beam size

Once you know Mmax, the required section modulus is S = M / Fb. Find S for any shape with the moment of inertia calculator, then check the bending stress: fb = M / S. Remember that serviceability (deflection, often limited to L/360 under live load) frequently controls before strength does.

FAQ

Why does the moment diagram look upside down?

The diagram is drawn on the tension side, the usual convention in structural engineering: positive (sagging) moment is plotted below the axis, where the bottom fiber is in tension.

Can it solve continuous beams or fixed–fixed beams?

Not yet. Those are statically indeterminate and need compatibility equations (or a matrix method). This tool covers simply supported beams, beams with overhangs and cantilevers.

Does it include the beam self-weight?

Only if you add it. Enter the weight per foot of the member as a uniform load over the full span.
