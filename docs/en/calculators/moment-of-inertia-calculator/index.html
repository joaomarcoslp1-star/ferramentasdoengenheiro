Moment of Inertia Calculator: Centroid, Section Modulus and Radius of Gyration

Updated 2026-11-27 · Section properties

Try to bend a ruler lying flat: easy. Now try to bend it standing on its edge: almost impossible. Same material, same area. What changes is the moment of inertia, the property that measures how far the area sits from the bending axis. The farther the material from the center, the stiffer the member. That is why beams are shaped like an I.

This calculator finds the centroid, the moments of inertia Ix and Iy, the section moduli and the radii of gyration of rectangles, solid circles, pipes, rectangular tubes, I shapes, T sections, angles, channels, or any section built from rectangles, showing the calculation part by part.

Step by step: centroid and the parallel axis theorem

For built-up sections (T, I, L, C or any combination of rectangles), the calculation takes three steps:

1. Split the section into simple parts (rectangles, circles) and write down each area Ai and the position of its centroid (xi, yi). Holes enter with a negative area.

2. Locate the centroid: ȳ = Σ(Ai · yi) / ΣAi and x̄ = Σ(Ai · xi) / ΣAi

3. Move each part's inertia to the centroidal axis (parallel axis theorem): Ix = Σ (Ix,own + Ai · di²), where di = yi − ȳ

The A·d² term is always positive and is usually the largest one: it is the reason why moving material away from the center increases the inertia so much.

Worked example: T section

A T section with a 6 × 0.75 in flange and a 0.5 in stem, 8 in overall depth (the example loaded in the calculator).

Parts: stem 0.5 × 7.25 in → A = 3.625 in², y = 3.625 in; flange 6 × 0.75 in → A = 4.5 in², y = 7.625 in.
Centroid: ȳ = (3.625 × 3.625 + 4.5 × 7.625) / 8.125 = 5.84 in from the bottom.
Stem: I = 0.5 × 7.25³/12 = 15.88 in⁴; d = 3.625 − 5.84 = −2.215 in; A·d² = 17.79 in⁴.
Flange: I = 6 × 0.75³/12 = 0.21 in⁴; d = 1.785 in; A·d² = 14.34 in⁴.
Ix = 15.88 + 17.79 + 0.21 + 14.34 ≈ 48.2 in⁴.

Section modulus and radius of gyration

  • Elastic section modulus S = I / c, where c is the distance from the centroid to the extreme fiber. It gives the bending stress: fb = M / S. Get M from the beam calculator and S from this page to check a member.
  • Radius of gyration r = √(I / A) governs column buckling through the slenderness ratio KL/r. A column buckles about the axis with the smaller r.

Note: AISC uses S for the elastic section modulus and Z for the plastic modulus. European tables call the elastic modulus W (Wel).

FAQ

Why is a ruler on edge so much stiffer?

For a 1.2 × 0.12 in ruler, I flat = 1.2 × 0.12³/12 ≈ 0.00017 in⁴. On edge, I = 0.12 × 1.2³/12 ≈ 0.0173 in⁴: 100 times larger with the same area, because the height enters cubed.

What is the difference between moment of inertia and section modulus?

The moment of inertia (in⁴) controls stiffness, that is, deflection. The section modulus (in³) controls strength, the maximum bending stress. A beam can pass on stress and still fail on deflection.

What are the product of inertia and the principal axes?

For sections without an axis of symmetry, such as an angle, the product of inertia Ixy is not zero and the axes of maximum and minimum inertia are rotated. The calculator shows the principal moments I1, I2 and the angle θ. Single angles buckle about the minor principal axis.

Why don't my results match the AISC table exactly?

Rolled shapes have fillets between the web and the flanges that this calculator ignores, and AISC tabulates design dimensions. Differences are small; for design, use the published values.
